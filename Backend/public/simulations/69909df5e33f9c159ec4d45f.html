The Mathematical Foundation

To analyze motion at a university level, we utilize vector calculus to describe the position vector r(t). For a launch from origin at angle θ:

Velocity: v(t) = (v₀ cos θ)i + (v₀ sin θ - gt)j
Position: r(t) = (v₀ t cos θ)i + (v₀ t sin θ - ½gt²)j

The Equation of the Trajectory describes a downward-opening parabola in a vacuum:

y = x tan θ - (gx²) / (2v₀² cos² θ)

Advanced Nuance: Air Resistance

In realistic environments, drag forces are velocity-dependent, coupling the horizontal and vertical equations. They are no longer independent.

Quadratic Drag: Applicable at high Reynolds numbers (most macroscopic projectiles), where F_d = -½ C_d ρ A v² v̂.

Result: The trajectory loses its symmetry. The peak occurs later in horizontal distance, and the descent is significantly steeper than the ascent.
